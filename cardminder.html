Discover

Why credit card reminders?

Most people manage more than one credit card — each with a different bank, billing cycle, and due date. Keeping track of all of them mentally is easy to get wrong, and getting it wrong comes with real consequences: late payment fees, interest charges, and damage to your credit score.

Beyond missed payments, there's another friction point that goes overlooked — accessing card details for online purchases. Without your physical card to hand, entering a card number, CVV, or expiry date from memory becomes a frustrating guessing game. I wanted to design an app that solves both problems in one place.

Users and existing apps

Based on research into how people manage their credit cards today, these were the most common frustrations users shared:

"I missed a payment last month because I forgot the due date. The late fee was more than my minimum payment."

"I have three credit cards with different billing dates. I can never remember which one is due when."

"Every time I shop online I have to go find my wallet just to check my CVV number."

"My bank app only shows my cards with them. I have no single place to see all my cards together."

Pain points

After reviewing how users manage their credit cards day-to-day, four core pain points emerged:

01

Missed Payment Due Dates

Users with multiple credit cards struggle to track different billing cycles and due dates. A single missed payment can trigger late fees and negatively impact credit scores.

02

No Advance Warning

Banking apps show a due date but don't proactively remind users ahead of time. By the time users notice, it's often too late to avoid a late payment charge.

03

Card Details Hard to Access

When shopping online without a physical card nearby, users have no quick way to retrieve their card number, CVV, or expiry date — causing friction at checkout.

04

Scattered Across Multiple Apps

Each bank has its own app. Users with cards from different banks have no single view of all their cards, billing cycles, and upcoming payments in one place.

Define

Problem statement

Cardminder is a mobile app designed for people who manage multiple credit cards and want a reliable, stress-free way to stay on top of payment due dates. Users can add their card details and billing cycle, and the app will remind them a set number of days before each payment is due — so no due date is ever missed.

Beyond reminders, Cardminder also serves as a secure quick-reference for card details. Users can view their card number, cardholder name, CVV, and expiry date at any time — and copy them directly to their clipboard for fast, friction-free use at online checkouts.

What Cardminder solves

Three core features directly address the problems users face when managing multiple credit cards:

Smart Payment Reminders

Users set how many days before the due date they want to be reminded. Cardminder sends a notification at exactly the right time — no more surprise late fees.

All Cards in One Place

Add any credit card regardless of the bank. View all your cards, billing cycles, and upcoming due dates together in a single, clear dashboard.

Copyable Card Details

View your card number, cardholder name, CVV, and expiry date at any time. Tap any field to copy it instantly — no more hunting for your physical card when shopping online.

Develop

Sitemap & user flows

I mapped out the core tasks users would need to perform — adding a card with its billing cycle, checking upcoming due dates, viewing card details, and managing reminder preferences. Keeping every flow as short as possible was the guiding principle throughout.

These flows helped surface where extra steps were adding unnecessary friction — particularly in the add-card flow, where asking for too many fields upfront could cause users to drop off.

1

Add a New Credit Card

Enter card details (card number, cardholder name, CVV, expiry) and set the billing cycle and due date. The app saves the card and schedules reminders automatically.

2

View Upcoming Payments

The home screen surfaces cards with payments due soon, ordered by urgency — so users always know what's coming up without having to dig through settings.

3

Access & Copy Card Details

Tap any card to view its full details. Each field — card number, CVV, expiry, cardholder name — has a copy button so users can paste directly into any online checkout.

4

Edit Card or Reminder Settings

Update card details, adjust the billing cycle, or change how many days in advance the reminder is sent — keeping everything accurate as cards and payment dates change.

Inspiration to prototype

I used Mobbin and existing finance and reminder apps to study how successful products handle sensitive data display and notification-driven interfaces. The goal was to apply familiar, trusted patterns — card-style layouts, tap-to-copy interactions, and clear urgency indicators — in a focused, purpose-built context.

Using established patterns reduced the learning curve for new users, which matters especially for a finance app where users need to feel confident and in control from the very first session.

The design decisions made in early wireframes — particularly the card detail layout and the reminder lead-time setting — carried through to the final high-fidelity screens with minimal change, which validated the direction early research had set.

The design system

I wanted Cardminder to feel trustworthy, calm, and in control — qualities that are essential for an app handling sensitive financial information. The colour palette centres on a confident blue for CTAs and key interactions, paired with clean whites and dark text to keep the focus on what matters: the cards and their due dates.

Iconography

Icons follow a consistent outlined style — simple, recognisable, and sized to meet the 48×48px touch target minimum to ensure comfortable interaction across all screen sizes.

Components

Core UI components include the credit card tile, top and bottom navigation bars, a floating action button for adding cards, specialised input fields (card number, CVV, expiry date, billing cycle), a date picker, primary buttons with a "Paid" variant, billing and reminder chips, and a toggle — each built to scale consistently across all screens.

Accessibility standards

Touch targets

All touch targets are a minimum of 48px by 48px. Where possible, touch targets span the full width of the screen to maximise ease of use — especially important when the app is used quickly at a till.

Color contrast

Every colour combination in the brand palette was tested for contrast to ensure all text and interactive elements meet WCAG AA standards for readability.

Color-blind safe

The brand colour palette was checked against common colour-blindness types to confirm no critical information is conveyed through colour alone.

Text sizing

Body text is set at or above 14px and label text at or above 12px bold. Line spacing is set to 150% to support comfortable reading across age groups and screen sizes.

Font selection

Inter was chosen for its excellent legibility at small sizes and its widespread familiarity across digital products. JetBrains Mono is used exclusively for card numbers and sensitive data fields — its fixed-width characters make long digit strings significantly easier to read and verify at a glance.

Deliver

High fidelity prototypes

Once the core flows were solid, I brought the app to life through high-fidelity design. The focus throughout was on making every screen feel calm, trustworthy, and purposeful — particularly the card detail screen, where sensitive information needs to feel secure yet instantly accessible.

Takeaways

Designing Cardminder reinforced that the best utility apps solve a specific, real problem — and solve it completely. A focused scope made every design decision clearer.

Proactive design beats reactive design

Showing users what's coming — rather than waiting for them to check — is what makes a reminder app genuinely useful. The home screen's urgency-ordered list was the single most important design decision in the project.

Trust is earned through detail

For a finance app, small decisions — clear typography, a calm colour palette, and an uncluttered layout — communicate reliability. Users need to feel the app is as careful with their data as they are.

Copyable data removes real friction

Adding tap-to-copy to individual card fields turned a pain point (re-typing card details at checkout) into a one-tap interaction. Simple affordances can have an outsized impact on everyday usability.

What's next

Explore a home screen widget for at-a-glance payment reminders. Add the ability to mark a payment as done to clear it from the upcoming list. Consider a spending notes field for each billing cycle.
