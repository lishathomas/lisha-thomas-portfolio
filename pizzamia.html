Discover

Why pizza lovers?

Pizza is one of the most well-known foods globally, yet users are not kept informed about what happens from the moment they order to the moment it arrives at their door. Not every pizza app makes that experience feel seamless or satisfying.

As a frequent user of food delivery apps, I noticed that ordering pizza — despite being one of the most popular fast food choices — often involves clunky interfaces, unclear customisation options, and slow checkout flows. I wanted to fix that.

What users were saying

Based on research on pizza lovers and existing pizza delivery apps, these were the recurring frustrations:

"I just want to build my pizza quickly, but I have to scroll forever and I'm never sure what I'm picking."

"I didn't realize that cheese burst and toppings added so much. The price just shot up at checkout!"

"I order the same pizza every time — why can't the app remember my favorite combo?"

"There's probably a coupon that fits my order, but I have no idea where to find it or if it even applies."

Pain points identified

After reviewing how users interact with popular pizza apps, four core pain points emerged:

01

Confusing Customisation Experience

Apps show long ingredient lists during customisation without visual feedback. Users scroll endlessly and are never sure what they're selecting.

02

Unclear Pricing & Add-ons

None of the existing apps show updated pricing during the topping selection stage. Pricing for add-ons only appears at checkout — a nasty surprise.

03

Hard-to-Find Deals & Offers

Offers are listed on banners but don't auto-match with cart contents. Users miss discounts that should apply automatically.

04

Lack of Personalisation & Smart Suggestions

No app saves favourite pizza builds or auto-suggests based on past orders. Repeat customers have to start from scratch every time.

Problem statement

Pizzamia is an online pizza ordering app intended to serve customers who want to enjoy pizza but cannot visit a restaurant due to time or convenience constraints. The app aims to ensure that the user experience during the ordering process is as positive as possible — so users continue to use it, find their favourite dishes, and recommend it to others.

The aim is to walk users through the complete ordering process in a way that feels intuitive, fast, and satisfying from first tap to order confirmation.

Competitor analysis

To understand the current landscape, I reviewed popular pizza-ordering apps including Domino's (India), Pizza Hut, OvenStory, Mojo Pizza, and the Zomato pizza ordering experience. My goal was to identify gaps that Pizzamia could address.

What Pizzamia solves

Three core features address the biggest drop-off points in the ordering experience:

Search & Filters

Predictive search and smart filters help users navigate and find exactly what they need — fast.

Flexible Payments

Net banking, credit/debit cards, and cash on delivery — users pay the way they prefer.

Live Order Tracking

Users can track their order at every stage, from kitchen to doorstep — no guessing required.

Define

Sitemap & user flows

I started by sketching out the main structure of the app, focusing on what users would actually need first. Looking at how similar apps are organised helped me shape a simple sitemap, which guided my wireframes, design style, and accessibility choices as I brought the idea to life.

These flows helped identify what felt natural to users — and what needed refinement to turn a casual craving into a seamless digital experience.

1

Explore & Choose

Browsing the menu and selecting a pizza — with visual previews and smart category filters.

2

Customise Order

Adding toppings with real-time price updates and visual ingredient feedback — so users always know what they're getting.

3

Apply Offers

Coupons are auto-matched to cart contents, and clearly surfaced before checkout — no hunting required.

4

Checkout & Track

Smooth payment flow followed by live order tracking from kitchen to doorstep.

Define

The design system

A strong design system takes the burden off individual designers to think through commonly recurring design problems. I started the brand design process by searching for food ordering branding examples — looking for something warm, motivating, but not intimidating.

I used Mobbin to find inspiration for specific pieces on every screen. Using existing design patterns made the product feel immediately familiar while still feeling fresh and distinctly Pizzamia.

Develop

High fidelity screens

Once the core flows felt solid, I brought the app to life through high-fidelity design. I wanted the experience to feel simple, familiar, and a little bit fun — just like ordering pizza should be.

Deliver

My learnings

I gained a lot of knowledge while developing this mobile app, realising that the initial thoughts are never the final ones. Every iteration taught me something new about how real users think about food ordering.

Research first, design second

Spending time on competitive research before opening Figma saved hours of rework. Understanding where existing apps fail gave me a clear design direction from day one.

Real-time feedback reduces anxiety

Showing live price updates and ingredient previews during customisation dramatically reduced the "checkout shock" users described. Small interactions carry big trust.

Familiarity is a feature

Using Mobbin to ground design decisions in established patterns meant users felt at home immediately. Good UX often borrows what already works — then improves on it.

What's next

Add nutrition details to menu items. Explore a loyalty/rewards system. Improve the order tracking map with live driver location for a more satisfying post-order experience.
