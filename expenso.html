Discover

Why an expense app?

Managing money can be stressful, especially when small expenses slip through the cracks. I wanted to design an app that makes tracking daily spending, budgeting, and financial insights simple, clear, and even a little motivating.

People love spending on things they love — but they're also concerned about how much they're spending. Unfortunately, not everyone has the time to track their expenses carefully. Expenso helps users track their spending and make more informed financial decisions.

What users were saying

From talking to users, I found that managing daily expenses often feels messy and overwhelming. Many forget to log small purchases, struggle to track budgets, or miss recurring payments.

"I forget to log small purchases, so my budget is never accurate."

"I can't quickly see if I'm staying within my budget!"

"Existing apps feel cluttered and confusing."

"I don't get reminders for recurring bills or subscriptions."

Pain points identified

Based on research on expense trackers and existing expense management apps, four core pain points emerged:

01

Tedious Manual Entry

Expensify requires users to scan or manually log every small transaction. Frequent users drop off due to the effort involved in logging every purchase.

02

Confusing or Rigid Categories

Zoho offers many preset categories, but customisation feels buried and unintuitive. Pockett has very few categories with limited ways to adjust them.

03

Overwhelming or Cluttered UI

Expensify presents too many stats and business-focused features, intimidating casual users. Zoho's dashboard is heavy with charts and tabs — newcomers struggle to find basic expense logging.

04

Privacy & Trust Concerns

SMS-based apps require sensitive permissions, raising privacy worries. Automated categorisation sometimes misreads promo SMS as expenses, eroding trust in the data.

Problem statement

Most expense trackers feel either too complicated or too limited. Expenso bridges this gap by focusing on what really matters — quick entry, clear insights, and personal control. The app is intended to help users who want to stay on top of their finances without having to become power users of a complex accounting tool.

Expenso will be a helpful tool for those looking to manage their finances and daily routines more effectively — turning expense tracking from a chore into a habit.

What Expenso solves

With these features, Expenso transforms expense tracking into a tool that supports smarter decisions and healthier financial habits.

Fast Logging

Add expenses in just a few taps — no clutter, no unnecessary steps. Quick-add from the home screen keeps the habit effortless.

Smart Categories

Flexible tagging that adapts to how each user actually spends — not rigid presets that force expenses into the wrong bucket.

Check Trends

Weekly and monthly spending trends shown as clear, visual charts — so users can instantly spot where their money is going.

Define

Sitemap & user flows

To make sure the app feels intuitive, I tested key flows that reflect how users manage money daily. The focus was on adding expenses quickly, tracking budgets, viewing trends, and staying on top of recurring payments.

1

Log an expense or income

Quick-add button for fast entry with fields for amount, category, date, and notes — designed to take under 10 seconds.

2

Check category-wise spending & budget progress

Dashboard overview with visual progress bars and category breakdowns — users see at a glance if they're on track.

3

View weekly or monthly trend reports

Trend graphs with filters by time and category — highlights of top spending areas or unusual expenses surface automatically.

4

Set budgets & receive overspend alerts

Users set budget limits per category, and receive smart notifications before they overspend — not after.

5

Manage recurring bills & subscriptions

A dedicated recurring payments tracker with reminders, so no subscription or bill is ever missed.

The design system

I wanted Expenso to feel clean, friendly, and trustworthy — the opposite of the heavy, business-focused tools I had analysed. The colour palette centres on a warm orange for CTAs and key interactions, paired with a near-white background and dark grey text for readability.

Deliver

My learnings

This project taught me that financial apps require an extra layer of trust-building — every design decision has to feel safe, clear, and honest. Complexity that helps power users can alienate everyday users entirely.

Simplicity is a strategy

Removing features that most users won't use made the core experience significantly stronger. The hardest design decision was what to leave out.

Visual feedback builds trust

Colour-coded categories and progress bars gave users instant clarity on their spending — no mental math required. Small visual cues carry a lot of weight in finance apps.

Reduce friction at the point of entry

The biggest drop-off in expense apps happens when logging feels like work. Making the add-expense flow as fast as possible was the single most impactful design priority.

What's next

Explore a receipt scanning feature for automatic entry. Add a savings goals module. Improve accessibility for users managing money under stress — colour alone should never be the only indicator.
