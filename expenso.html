Discover

Why an expense app?

Managing money can be stressful, especially when small expenses slip through the cracks. I wanted to design an app that makes tracking daily spending, budgeting, and financial insights simple, clear, and even a little motivating.

Users & existing apps

From talking to users, I found that managing daily expenses often feels messy and overwhelming. Many forget to log small purchases, struggle to track budgets, or miss recurring payments. This inspired me to design an app that keeps finances simple and under control.

"I forget to log small purchases, so my budget is never accurate."

"I can't quickly see if I'm staying within my budget!"

"Existing apps feel cluttered and confusing."

"I don't get reminders for recurring bills or subscriptions."

Pain points

Based on research on expense trackers and existing expense management apps, four core pain points emerged:

01

Tedious Manual Entry

Expensify requires users to scan or manually log every small transaction. Frequent users drop off due to the effort involved.

02

Confusing or Rigid Categories

Zoho offers many preset categories, but customisation feels buried and unintuitive. Pockett has very few categories with limited ways to adjust them.

03

Overwhelming or Cluttered UI

Rich charts and reports can overwhelm casual users. Multi-layered dashboards with detailed analytics confuse new users who just want a simple overview.

04

Privacy & Trust Concerns

Linking bank accounts for full automation raises trust concerns. Strong security but permissions can feel intrusive — users want control over their data.

Define

Problem statement

People love spending on items they love. They are also concerned about how much they are spending. Unfortunately, not everyone has the time to track their expenses. Therefore Expenso can help its users to track their expenses and help them make more informed financial decisions.

With these, Expenso transforms expense tracking into a tool that supports smarter decisions and healthier financial habits.

Competitor analysis

To better understand the market, I reviewed popular expense tracking apps like Expensify, Zoho Expense, Pockett, and SMS-based trackers. While each offers useful features, I found common gaps around simplicity, personalisation, and trust.

Feature Expensify Zoho Expense Pockett
Ease of logging ❌ Manual & tedious ❌ Complex setup ✓ Quick manual entry
Category flexibility ❌ Buried customisation ❌ Not obvious ❌ Very limited
Insight visualisation ❌ Overwhelming ❌ Confusing ✓ Easy to scan
Budget alerts ❌ Business-focused ❌ Manual setup ❌ None
Data privacy ❌ Bank link required ❌ Intrusive permissions ✓ Manual, preserved

What Expenso app solves

With these insights, Expenso was designed to directly address the four core pain points identified in research:

01

Tedious Manual Entry

Quick-add from the home screen makes logging an expense a matter of seconds — no menus, no friction.

02

Confusing or Rigid Categories

Flexible tagging that adapts to how each user actually spends — not rigid presets that force expenses into the wrong bucket.

03

Overwhelming or Cluttered UI

A clean, focused dashboard with only what users need at a glance — spending totals, budget progress, and recent transactions.

04

Privacy & Trust Concerns

No bank account linking required. All data stays on the user's device, giving full control and peace of mind.

Develop

Sitemap & user flows

To make sure the app feels intuitive, I tested key flows that reflect how users manage money daily. The focus was on adding expenses quickly, tracking budgets, viewing trends, and staying on top of recurring payments.

Sitemap showing user flow at various levels: Red — primary, Blue — secondary, Yellow — screen with other options, Green — final screen.

1

Log a new expense or income quickly

Quick-add button for fast entry with fields for amount, category, date, and notes — designed to take under 10 seconds.

2

Check category-wise spending & budget progress

Dashboard overview with visual progress bars and category breakdowns — users see at a glance if they're on track.

3

View weekly or monthly trend reports

Trend graphs with filters by time and category — highlights of top spending areas or unusual expenses surface automatically.

4

Set budgets & receive alerts for overspending

Users set budget limits per category and receive smart notifications before they overspend — not after.

5

Manage recurring bills or subscriptions

A dedicated recurring payments tracker with reminders, so no subscription or bill is ever missed.

Inspiration to prototype

Once the flows were solid, I brought the app to life with a clean, friendly design. I focused on making it easy for users to log expenses, see their budgets at a glance, and track trends without feeling overwhelmed.

Visual cues, colour-coded categories, and simple charts replaced the cluttered interfaces I had analysed. Using existing design patterns helped the product feel immediately familiar while still feeling fresh and distinctly Expenso.

The design system

I wanted Expenso to feel clean, friendly, and trustworthy — the opposite of the heavy, business-focused tools I had analysed. The colour palette centres on a warm orange for CTAs and key interactions, paired with a near-white background and dark text for readability.

Iconography

Components

Core UI components include CTA buttons and card layouts — each built to scale consistently across all screens.

Accessibility standards

Touch targets

All touch targets are a minimum of 48px by 48px. And I looked for opportunities for touch targets to span the width of the screen.

Color contrast

Every color combination of the brand palette was tested for color contrast to make sure we only used combinations that met WCAG's AA contrast criteria.

Color-blind safe

The brand color palette was checked for possible color-blind conflicts.

Text sizing

Longer paragraph text sizing is set at or above 14px and label text is set at or above 12px bold. Line spacing was set to 150% for easier readability.

Font selection

Poppins was chosen as the font is simple and clear to everyone.

Takeaways

I gained a lot of knowledge while developing this mobile app, realising that the initial thoughts are only the start of the process and that user research input should inform each iteration and the degree of prototyping.

Research informs every iteration

Initial ideas are just a starting point. Each round of user feedback reshaped priorities and surfaced needs I hadn't anticipated at the start.

Simplicity is a strategy

Removing features that most users won't use made the core experience significantly stronger. The hardest design decision was what to leave out.

Validate pain points with real users

I will be conducting another round of usability studies to validate the pain points of users while experiencing the app.

What next

Use learnings from this project to continue developing my skills as a UX Designer. Will also conduct more user research to determine any new areas of need.
