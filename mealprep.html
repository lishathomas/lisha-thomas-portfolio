Discover

The daily meal decision problem

Most people struggle with deciding what to eat every day. The questions "what's for breakfast?" and "what should I cook for dinner?" repeat every single day — causing decision fatigue, wasted time, and unhealthy last-minute choices.

The problem isn't a lack of recipes. It's the absence of a simple system to plan ahead — one built around your own meals, not a stranger's recipe database.

Target users

Weekli is built for anyone who regularly decides what to cook — and wants to do it with less effort and more intention:

01

Homemakers

Managing weekly family meals — often the sole person responsible for planning what the household eats every day.

02

Couples

Planning meals together and wanting a shared, at-a-glance view of the week so neither person is asking "what's for dinner?" every night.

03

Individuals

Living alone and wanting to eat intentionally — reusing meals they enjoy rather than defaulting to takeout when decision fatigue hits.

04

Busy professionals

Planning the week ahead on Sunday so that food decisions are already made and don't compete with work-week mental bandwidth.

Define

Problem statement

People don't need more recipes — they already know what they like to cook. What they need is a simple, structured way to plan which meals they'll make each day of the week, using the meals they already know and enjoy.

Weekli was designed around a single idea: your recipes, organised by meal type, mapped to your week. No recipe database. No nutrition tracking. Just a calm, focused planning tool that helps you decide ahead of time — so you never face the question "what's for dinner?" unprepared again.

What Weekli is designed around

Weekli is designed around one goal — making weekly meal planning as effortless as possible. Every feature is shaped by the habits and needs identified in research, with a focus on simplicity, personal ownership of recipes, and a calm, inclusive experience.

01

Your recipes, your library

No built-in recipe database to wade through. Users build a library of the meals they actually cook — and it grows more useful every week they use it.

02

Categorised by meal type

When planning Breakfast, only Breakfast recipes appear. Each slot surfaces exactly what's relevant — no scrolling, no irrelevant suggestions.

03

A week at a glance

Seven days, three meals per day — all visible and editable in one view. Plan on Sunday, and the week's decisions are already made.

04

Pinned meal types

Users can pin a meal type — Breakfast, Lunch, or Dinner — to their favourite recipes. Pinned recipes surface at the top when planning that slot, making repeat choices even faster.

05

Auto weekly reset

Every Monday the planner resets to a clean empty week. No manual clearing — just open the app and start planning again.

Develop

Core features

Weekli V1.0 is built on two features. Everything else is out of scope until these are validated.

01

Recipe Library

  • Users build a personal collection of their own recipes
  • Organised into three categories: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner
  • Each recipe can be tagged to one or more meal types — Breakfast, Lunch, or Dinner
  • Users can also pin a recipe to a meal type, surfacing it at the top of that list for faster planning
  • Add, delete, and categorise recipes at any time
  • Accessible via the Meals tab in the bottom navigation
02

Meal Planner

  • 7-day scrollable calendar with Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner slots
  • Tap "+ Add" on any slot to assign a meal
  • Only relevant recipes shown per slot — Breakfast shows Breakfast recipes
  • Auto-resets to a new empty week every Monday
  • Home screen shows the full week plan at a glance

Add meal flow

The add meal experience differs depending on whether the user is new or returning — keeping the first-time experience as simple as possible, while giving returning users full control of their growing library.

First time user

Tap Add → Type → Done

User taps the Add CTA on any meal slot. A bottom sheet slides up with a simple text field — user types the meal name and taps Add. The meal is instantly added to that slot on the home screen. No library, no options, no friction.

Returning user — 3-option bottom sheet

Once the user has built up a recipe library, tapping "+ Add" opens a bottom sheet with three options — giving them control over planning, organising, and maintaining their library.

Add

Opens the recipe list filtered to the relevant meal type. User selects a saved recipe or types a new one to add to both the library and the meal slot.

Clean

Opens the recipe list with a delete option. User removes recipes they no longer want from their library.

See recipes

Opens the full recipe library. User can view all recipes, reassign categories, and tag or pin a recipe to a meal type.

Design principles

Five principles guided every design decision — from the information architecture to the smallest interaction:

Focused

One job, done well — plan your week with your own recipes. Nothing is added that doesn't serve that goal.

Personal

Your recipes, your meals, your week. The app has no opinion about what you should eat — it just helps you plan it.

Simple

Every core action takes 3 taps or fewer. If it takes more, the design needs to change — not the user.

Calm

No overwhelming features, no cluttered screens, no notifications demanding attention. Meal planning should feel easy.

Inclusive

Clean, minimal design that works for everyone — regardless of gender, background, or how they approach cooking.

3-tap rule

Adding a meal, cleaning the library, categorising a recipe — any core task must be reachable within 3 taps from the home screen.

The design system

The visual identity is grounded and natural — fresh greens to connect to food, a deep near-black for text, and an off-white background that keeps the focus on the content. Deliberately gender-neutral: no pinks, no florals, no stock photography of salads.

Iconography

Icons follow a rounded, consistent style — friendly without being playful. All icons are paired with labels where space allows, and meet a minimum 48×48px touch target across all screens.

Components

Core components include the day column in the weekly planner, the meal slot card, the bottom sheet, the recipe list row, and the floating add button. Each is designed to be immediately scannable and thumb-friendly in a one-handed hold.

Accessibility standards

Touch targets

All interactive elements are a minimum of 48×48px. Meal slot cards and recipe list rows span the full screen width for easy one-thumb tapping.

Color contrast

All text and icon pairings meet WCAG AA contrast standards. Green is used only for interactive and active elements — never as the sole indicator of meaning.

Color-blind safe

Planned vs. empty meal slots are differentiated by both colour and icon — the distinction is never colour alone.

Text sizing

Body text is 16px minimum. Labels and captions are 13px bold or above. Line spacing is set to 150% for comfortable reading in a handheld context.

Font selection

DM Sans was chosen for its clean legibility at all sizes — particularly at the small sizes used in the 7-day planner grid.

Out of scope — V1.0

Weekli V1.0 is intentionally focused. The following features are not included in the initial release — and that's a deliberate design decision, not an oversight:

Grocery list generation
Budget tracking and shopping mode
Weekly reminders and push notifications
Recipe database or recipe suggestions
Calorie or nutrition tracking
Social sharing of meal plans
AI generated meal suggestions
Multi-user or family sharing

Takeaways

Weekli reinforced a lesson that's easy to intellectualise but hard to practice: the discipline of saying no. The instinct was to add grocery lists, budget tracking, and reminders. The decision was to ship one thing first — and ship it well.

Scope is a design decision

Choosing V1.0 as recipe library + meal planner only wasn't a limitation — it was a strategy. A focused product that earns trust is the foundation for everything that comes after.

Design for yourself, validate with others

Starting from a personal pain point gave the problem immediate clarity and conviction. The test was whether others shared it — and research confirmed they do.

The bottom sheet unlocked simplicity

Putting Add, Clean, and See recipes behind a single "+ Add" tap kept the planner view completely uncluttered — all the functionality is there, none of it is in the way.

What next

Conduct usability testing on the V1.0 prototype. Validate the recipe library flow and the weekly planning experience before designing grocery list and budget features for V2.0.
